# SkillBridge app walkthrough: click and explain

Read the quoted text while performing the actions above it. Start signed in on **Your starting point**. Allow about 4 minutes, with most quiz answering trimmed or sped up. Bracketed notes are instructions for you, not lines to read aloud.

## 1. Introduce the app

**On screen:** Keep the app name and preferences page visible.

> “This is SkillBridge, a financial literacy learning platform for high school students. It helps students find a useful starting point by combining what they want to learn with a short check of what they already know. I'll walk through how a student uses it.”

## 2. Choose interests

**Click:** Under **Your starting point**, select **Budgeting** and **Saving**. If already selected, just point to them.

> “First, the student creates a learning profile. They can choose interests such as budgeting, saving, credit, and investing. For this example, I'm choosing budgeting and saving.”

## 3. Enter a goal and experience level

**Do:** Enter **I want to plan a spending budget and build emergency savings.** Choose **Beginner**. Pause on the completed form.

> “Next, they describe their learning goal and select their experience level. I'm using a beginner who wants to manage spending and build emergency savings. The app uses these preferences to help match resources to their interests and choose an appropriate difficulty.”

**Click:** **Save and continue**. Wait for the knowledge check page.

> “Saving keeps these preferences on the student's account and takes them to the knowledge check. They can come back and update their preferences later.”

## 4. Demonstrate the assessment

**Click:** **Start knowledge check**, or **Take the assessment again** if a saved result is already displayed.

> “The knowledge check has ten questions covering budgeting, saving, credit, and investing. It gives the app some evidence about which topics the student may benefit from practicing.”

**Do:** Select an answer, click **Next**, then click **Previous** to show the selection is preserved. Point to the numbered question buttons and answered count.

> “Students select one answer per question. They can move forward, go back, or jump to a question using these numbers. Their selections stay in place as they review the quiz, and the progress indicator shows how many questions they've answered.”

**Do:** Finish all ten questions. Trim or speed up most of this answering in the final video.

**Click:** **Submit assessment** once, then wait for scoring to finish.

> “Once all ten questions are answered, the student submits the assessment. Scoring happens on the server, and the completed result is saved to their account.”

## 5. Explain the results

**On screen:** Show the overall score, then scroll to the full **Performance by topic** table.

> “Here are the saved results. The overall score gives a quick summary, while this table breaks performance down by topic. That helps distinguish areas where the student answered confidently from areas where more practice may help. This is a short diagnostic, so it's a starting point rather than a complete measure of financial knowledge.”

[Optional: point to one actual topic and say its correct/total count. Use the result currently visible.]

## 6. Explain how recommendations work

**Click:** **Continue to recommendations**. Wait for the page to load. Keep **How these suggestions work** visible.

> “SkillBridge now ranks learning resources using the student's saved preferences and latest completed assessment. It's an explainable, rule-based system. Knowledge-gap relevance contributes 45 percent of the ranking score, difficulty compatibility contributes 35 percent, and interests and learning-goal keywords contribute 20 percent.”

**Do:** Scroll to the ranked resource cards.

> “Together, those factors help balance what the student wants to learn, what fits their experience level, and where their assessment suggests more practice could help. The app shows up to five recommended resources.”

## 7. Walk through one resource card

**On screen:** Stop on one complete card. Point to its title, provider, topic, difficulty, resource type, ranking score, and explanation as you mention them.

> “Each card shows the resource's title and provider, along with its topic, difficulty, and format. The ranking score shows how it compares with other resources for this student; it isn't a probability of success. Underneath, the explanation describes the factors behind that recommendation, so the student can see why it appears in their list.”

[Pause briefly on the explanation. If you want a specific example, summarize what this card actually says.]

**Click:** **Open Resource**. Briefly show the destination, then return to the SkillBridge tab.

> “When they find something useful, they can open the original resource in a new tab and start learning.”

## 8. Demonstrate the library

**Click:** **Explore resources**. Show the count and cards.

> “Students can also explore the resource library independently. This gives them a way to find something specific beyond their personalized recommendations.”

**Do:** Search for a word from a visible resource title or description that you tested beforehand.

> “Search narrows the list using words in resource titles and descriptions.”

**Do:** Select a **Topic** and **Difficulty** combination that returns results. Point to **Resource type**, or select a matching type if available.

> “The filters can work together. A student can narrow the library by topic, choose a difficulty, and select a resource type. The results update as they adjust these controls.”

**Click:** **Clear Filters**.

> “Clear Filters removes the search and filter selections and brings back the full available library.”

## 9. Show saved progress and explain retakes

**Click:** **Knowledge check**. Show the saved result. Refresh this completed-results page and wait for it to reload.

> “The student doesn't have to start over when they return. Their latest completed result is saved. They can also retake the assessment, and recommendations will use the new topic results the next time they open For you.”

**Click:** **Your starting point**. Briefly show the saved preferences, then click **For you** and wait for recommendations.

> “Their learning preferences are saved too, and can change as their goals change. SkillBridge brings those goals, the knowledge check, and relevant resources together to give the student a clear next step.”

## Recording reminders

- Pause your narration during loading instead of describing content before it appears.
- Keep one recommendation explanation readable for several seconds.
- Capture real results; don't promise a particular score or ranking order.
- Complete the quiz before navigating away. Refresh only after the result is saved.
- If you need a shorter video, omit the external-resource visit and the final refresh, and shorten the recommendation-weight explanation.
